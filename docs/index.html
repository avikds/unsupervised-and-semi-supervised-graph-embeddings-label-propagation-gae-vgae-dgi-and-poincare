Unsupervised and Semi-Supervised Graph Embeddings: Label Propagation, GAE/VGAE, DGI and Poincare

How it was built

Part 1: Graphs with a Known Truth

Two graphs the whole project scores against. A degree-corrected contextual stochastic block model gives labels, Gaussian node features and the true edge probabilities, so every link predictor can be compared with the best possible one. A balanced tree gives a metric that hyperbolic space should fit and Euclidean space should not.

Part 2: Labels as Embeddings

The simplest GraphEDM methods use the label space itself as the embedding and the identity as the decoder. You build label propagation by iteration and in closed form (eq 23.19), watch one class swallow the graph when only 1% of nodes are labeled, find out why, repair it with class mass normalisation, and compare label spreading (eq 23.20).

Part 3: Shallow Embeddings Without Training

Two ways to get a lookup table of node vectors from linear algebra alone. Laplacian eigenmaps solve the generalised eigenproblem of eq 23.8, and you see what one tiny component does to them. DeepWalk's random-walk statistics have a closed form (eq 23.17, whose printed weights you derive and correct), and factorising it gives skip-gram embeddings without ever sampling a walk. You score communities with K-means and the adjusted Rand index.

Part 4: Graph Auto-Encoders

Now the encoder is a GCN and the graph decoder is an inner product. You write one trainer that is literally the GraphEDM loss of eq 23.4, a GCN encoder, a leak-free link split, and the two forms of the reconstruction loss (eq 23.31). Then you train GAE and VGAE (eq 23.32-23.33), score them against the true edge probabilities, and sweep the KL weight to find where the variational term helps and where the posterior collapses.

Part 5: Deep Graph Infomax and the Linear Probe

A contrastive alternative to reconstruction: teach a discriminator to tell real node embeddings from those of a graph with shuffled features (eq 23.34). You judge it with a linear probe on very few labels, against its own untrained encoder and against plain feature smoothing that needs no training at all.

Part 6: Using Labels: GCN, the Joint Loss and Planetoid

Turn on alpha. A GCN classifier keeps the graph in the encoder, and you test whether adding the reconstruction term of eq 23.4 helps it. Planetoid keeps the graph out of the feature encoder and uses it only in the loss, with context pairs sampled from the DeepWalk matrix (eq 23.35-23.36). You compare joint training with the two-stage pipeline it was meant to replace.

Part 7: Poincare Against Euclid on a Tree

The chapter's claim that hyperbolic space suits hierarchies, tested on a balanced tree at equal dimension. You write the Poincare distance (eq 23.11), the softmax loss of eq 23.12, Riemannian SGD with projection back into the ball, and the same trainer in both geometries. Then you score reconstruction by mean rank, mean average precision and distortion, with classical MDS (eq 23.7) as the closed-form baseline.

Part 8: The GraphEDM Report

Run every experiment on one seed, collect the numbers, and place each method in the GraphEDM table: encoder, target, decoder, loss, and which of alpha, beta and gamma it switches on.
